## 1. Profile load: PASS

### Parse

I wrote my own parser (`parse_args.py`, in the scratchpad). It ignores empty lines and lines that start with `#` after spaces, splits each line into flag + value at the first space and refuses a value with a space. The environment comes from `runtime/llama-server.env`: uncommented `KEY=VALUE` lines, exported.

```
env: GGML_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH=1024
llama-server -m ~/models/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-UD-Q4_K_XL.gguf --host 127.0.0.1 --port 9931 \
  -a qwen3.6-35b -np 1 -c 131072 -fa on -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -ngl 99 -ncmoe 38 --fit on --fit-target 384 \
  -b 4096 -ub 4096 -t 8 -tb 12 -lm dio --jinja --reasoning-preserve -ctxcp 48 -cms 2048 \
  --cache-ram 12288 --metrics --temp 0.6 --top-p 0.95 --top-k 20
```

That is 28 tokens, identical to the summary in `RESULTADOS-E4.md`. `#-lv 4` was left out. The format is solid: no line has a value with a space.

### Time to `/health` (polling every 0.5 s)

| Load | Flags | `/health` 200 |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | exact profile | **30.3 s** |
| P5 | exact profile | **31.8 s** |

With the exact profile, the time matches the claim. Between consecutive loads it varies from 30 to 43 s (minor M3). The E5 unit waits up to 600 s (`wait-ready --timeout 600`), so the variation is irrelevant.

- The line `offloading 39 repeating layers to GPU` contradicts `n_layer = 40`, but `offloaded 41/41` and the buffers show everything on the GPU. It is cosmetic in llama.cpp.

## 2. VRAM with full context: PASS

Sampler `nvidia-smi.exe --query-gpu=timestamp,memory.used,memory.free,temperature.gpu,power.draw,utilization.gpu,pcie.link.gen.current,pcie.link.width.current,clocks_event_reasons.active -lms 1000`. There were 964 samples from 12:13 to 12:29.

- **Real 120k context:** a new conversation with 6 source files (117,611 tokens processed + 689 of system prefix and tools). Then came 2 warm turns and 2 generations of 320 tokens. The slot ended with `n_tokens = 120046` (log).
- **VRAM:** 6,718 MiB right after loading. It rose to 6,794 MiB on the first large batch and **stayed at 6,794 MiB in all 195 samples** of the 120k phase (minimum = maximum). It matches the builder's 6,794–6,796.
- **Headroom:**
  - `nvidia-smi`: 1,117 MiB free at minimum.
  - Server breakdown (`-lv 4`, on exit): `CUDA0 8150 = 472 free + (6571 = 3034 + 1422 + 2114) + 1106 unaccounted`. That is **472 MiB free** in CUDA's view, equal to the builder.
  - Both views are above ~300 MiB. The headroom that counts is CUDA's (472), because WDDM reserves ~1.1 GB that `nvidia-smi` shows as free.
- The breakdown was read in a separate load (P2), not during the 120k fill. The allocation is static: the KV of 131072 cells is reserved at load and the VRAM stayed flat. For this reason the value holds for the full context.
- **RAM:** RSS of 22.7 GiB at the end, with the 20k conversation still in the prompt cache (builder: peak of 22.5 GiB). `MemAvailable` of 29.3 GB right after loading.

## 5. The two surprises: PASS

`llama-bench` with the same flags as the profile, changing only the environment variable:

```
F="-m $M -fa 1 -ctk q8_0 -ctv q8_0 -ngl 99 -ncmoe 38 -ub 4096 -b 4096 -lm dio -t 8,12,16 -p 128,512 -n 128 -r 3 -o jsonl"
env -u GGML_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH llama-bench $F      # A: default (32), 12:23–12:25
env GGML_OP_OFFLOAD_MIN_BATCH=1024 llama-bench $F    # B: 1024,         12:25–12:26
```

| Threads | pp128 A → B | pp512 A → B | tg128 A / B |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 180.2 ± 7.2 → **396.1 ± 27.9** | 397.9 ± 136 (477/240/476) → **630.0 ± 43.7** | **37.6 ± 1.4 / 36.7 ± 0.4** |
| 12 | 182.0 ± 2.2 → **408.8 ± 19.5** | 473.1 ± 3.1 → 508.0 ± 204 (273/637/614) | 35.2 ± 1.0 / 35.4 ± 3.8 |
| 16 | 145.9 ± 59.6 → 398.1 ± 30.7 | 461.3 ± 5.6 → 578.5 ± 3.6 | **30.5 ± 0.7 / 30.1 ± 0.6** |

- **(a)** With `MIN_BATCH=1024`, pp128 becomes 2.2× faster and pp512 rises 23 to 33% (by the medians). The effect is real; the magnitude is smaller than the 2.7× in `RESULTADOS` and the "up to 2.5×" in the `.env` comment (M1). With `-ncmoe 38`, the default path yields 181 tok/s, not 141. The builder's number came from `-ncmoe 40` with Broadcast active.
- **(b)** `-t 16` loses 18 to 19% against `-t 8` on generation, and `-t 12` loses 4 to 6%. The effect is real, but the loss with 16 threads is slightly smaller than the claimed "20 to 30%" (M1).
- For the CPU path (`-tb`), 8 and 12 threads are close (~400 on pp128; pp512 between 614 and 637 without the outliers) and 16 is lower on pp512. `-tb 12` is reasonable.
- Each configuration had one isolated slow pp512 sample (240 and 273 against ~470 and ~620). It is the same intermittency as the builder's open question 3. I did not investigate it.

## Problems

None blocking, none important.

- **M1 (minor): magnitudes slightly inflated in three places.** The conclusions do not change.
  - The `.env` comment says "up to 2.5× (pp128: 384 against 142)"; I measured 2.2× (181 → 400) with the final profile.
  - `RESULTADOS`/`.args` say `-t 16` "drops ~25%" (20–30%); I measured 18–19%.
  - `--moe-cache-mib 1024` gives −36% for the builder; I measured −22%.
  - Suggestion: cite ranges ("2.2 to 2.7×", "−18 to −30%") or the condition of each number (the 142 is from `-ncmoe 40` with Broadcast active).
- **M2 (minor): at the default log level, the server does not show the VRAM headroom.**
  - Without `-lv 4`, there is no `--fit` line, no buffers and no memory breakdown, not even on exit.
  - The failure warning (`W ... failed to fit params ... abort`) does appear, so the verification planned for E5 (looking for `failed to fit params`) works.
  - To track the headroom in production, use `nvidia-smi` (≈6,794 MiB expected) or a diagnostic load with `-lv 4`.
  - As the builder already recorded, the warning does not protect against other apps on the GPU, because CUDA still sees 7,062 MiB free.
- **M3 (minor): the load time varies from 30 to 43 s between consecutive loads.** The exact profile took 30.3 and 31.8 s. "~33 s" is a good typical value, but not a ceiling. No impact, because the E5 unit waits up to 600 s.
- **M4 (minor, for whoever measures in E5/E6): warm-up.** The first 1–2 requests after loading generate ~10% slower (31.6 and 32.1 against 34–38 tok/s). Discard the first request in measurements.

## Conclusion

- The builder's central numbers were reproduced with independent texts, client and sampler, within ±5%. The ones that differ came out better (generation at 20k).
- The profile loads as written and keeps all attention on the GPU. VRAM stays flat at 6.8 GB up to 120k with 472 MiB free in CUDA's view, cache reuse between turns works and PCIe was stable.
- **The profile is fit for E5.**
